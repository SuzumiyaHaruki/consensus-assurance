# Shared behavior and semantic facts

Describe actual actors, triggers, contexts, guards, effects, branches and asynchronous handoffs. For a relationship already read and relevant to the question, identify who establishes the fact under which conditions, which other read paths establish/invalidate/reinterpret it, and who consumes it. Compare the consumer's required meaning with the producer's weakest guarantee, including owner, identity, context and completion boundaries. These are research questions using existing records, not mandatory fields or a completeness score.

An existing Behavior may cover several entry paths through a shared helper. Explain those paths and conditions within that abstraction when accurate; split only when owners or effects mean different things. Reuse a Fact for the same assertion and identity. A raw variable, notification, durable completion and completed authority transfer are not interchangeable guarantees. One guard explains its branch, not every producer or consumer. Preserve actual alternatives and uncertainty instead of merging stronger and weaker promises for connectivity.

Use produces_fact_ids/consumes_fact_ids for sourced relationships; reverse indexes are derived. Do not leave a relevant, already-established consumption solely in prose when later questions need its navigation. Do not infer edges from names, nearby code or general protocol knowledge. Unread paths remain unknown or sourced Surfaces; a Behavior can have no Fact edges. Counts, graph connectivity and passing reference validation do not establish semantic completeness.

Follow actual dependencies across identity, context versions, object replacement, persistence, configuration, recovery and independent loops. Compare alternate guards, ownership, serialization, validation and reconstruction. Different ordering or asymmetric code alone is not a defect; first establish what each path guarantees and what its consumer assumes.
