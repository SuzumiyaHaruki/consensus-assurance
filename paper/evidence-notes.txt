Canonical terminology
---------------------
System: Tanze (provisional name; Chinese name: 探赜).
Agent: the native Codex investigation session; the model need not be an OpenAI model.
Controller: the runtime responsible for records, isolation, execution, and
declared structural/observation checks.
Investigation: the sourced question, hypotheses, disposition, gaps, and history.
FixedExperiment: the exact executable artifact and declared checks.
Interpretation: the current attributed explanation, evidence, counterevidence,
gap answers, limitations, and scoped defect judgment.
Behavior: actual conditional execution.
Fact: what behavior establishes for an object and context under conditions.
Formal execution: execution of an accepted fixed artifact by the controller;
it does not mean formal verification.
Source-based negative: a scoped exclusion explained by source paths, not an
empirically measured absence or a proof of the implementation as a whole.

Argument and section allocation
-------------------------------
Central argument: source explanations and executable checks develop together.
Keeping the question, fixed observations, and current explanation distinct
allows the agent to revise premises and continue investigating.
Abstract: CFT consensus importance and defect consequences; growing use of LLMs and
the challenge of investigating complex systems; the Tanze method; an explicit
evaluation placeholder. The user requested this compact four-part structure,
drawing on Agora's rhetorical organization without copying its prose.
The former running-example result is omitted from the abstract; empirical
effectiveness will be written when the evaluation is ready.
The abstract foregrounds evidence-driven autonomous investigation (narrative B
in the second review). Consensus reasoning supplies the investigation direction:
leadership, membership and recovery can change decision premises and the progress
obligations of accepted operations. New evidence challenging a premise motivates
reassessment of dependent conclusions; accumulating unrelated evidence does not.
The mechanism combines source reasoning with isolated experiments to investigate
whether proposed violations can arise under calling and fault assumptions.
Isolation itself does not establish history validity. Questions, experiments and
interpretations remain distinct, with observations preserved as explanations
change. Unresolved premises and explicitly recorded links between premises and
conclusions guide further inquiry. Controller, session and execution-version
details remain in Design rather than occupying the abstract.
The agent selects affected claims and subsequent inquiry; the controller does
not establish semantic validity or infer unrecorded dependencies. The abstract
describes the records as helping the agent identify affected claims. Checking
the feasibility of a suspected violation's execution history can involve source
reasoning or experiments; a source-based resolution need not execute a distributed
scenario. The loop is not a mandatory pipeline.
The two investigation axes remain a search method, not safety/liveness verification
claims. The title and abstract explicitly scope the work to CFT consensus, with
databases and coordination services as motivation. Comparative effectiveness and case
results remain absent from the abstract until the evaluation is ready.
Introduction: CFT importance and cross-path interactions; LLM capabilities and
the evidence needed to establish a defect; the challenge of revising an account
as new evidence changes its premises; the Tanze method and evidence/continuation
mechanisms; three design contributions and an explicit evaluation placeholder.
The detailed motivating example and named-system literature survey are removed
from the Introduction. Brief citations support the background and capability
statements; detailed comparisons are reserved for a later independent Related
Work section. The existing Design examples remain illustrative rather than being
promoted to evaluation results. Protocol conditions determine the applicability
of implementation actions; investigative premises support the agent's explanation.
The text does not imply that a context change revokes an earlier valid commit,
that every question requires execution, or that the controller proves histories
reachable. Source evidence may resolve a suspicion directly. Recorded dependencies
help the agent select substantively affected claims for reconsideration.
Section 2: consensus and replicated state machines; safety and progress under
operating assumptions; LLM agents combining hypotheses, tests or models, and
execution feedback; the investigation challenge linking responsibility, history,
and observations as understanding changes.
Section 3: workflow and semantic owners; consensus relationships illustrated by
the recovery omission; executable history and endpoints of the same case;
revision and continued inquiry. Exact revisions and archive locations remain in
these notes rather than serving as the narrative opening of a case in the paper.
The figures express an observed causal sequence and the implemented workflow.
The mechanism table is explanatory, not a coverage or completeness claim.
