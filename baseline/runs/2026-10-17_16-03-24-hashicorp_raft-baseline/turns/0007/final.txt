The strongest new result is an executed Raft safety violation caused by Finding 1.

A node restarted with a stale snapshot configuration `{A}` while its committed log contained `{A,B,C}`. In a separate partition, the current-configuration side elected with `B,C`. Both sides then committed commands at the same log index and term:

```text
A:  term=2 data="from-stale-partition"    commit=4
BC: term=2 data="from-current-partition" commit=4
```

This violates Raft's Log Matching Property and election safety. The result reproduced consistently in three runs. Evidence is in `audit_results/finding1_stale_conflict.txt` and `audit_results/stale_conflict_run{1,2,3}.txt`.

The earlier stale-election test also showed a single stale `{A}` node electing and committing a no-op despite a committed `{A,B}` configuration. Together these move Finding 1 beyond availability: the configuration-recovery defect can produce two leaders and conflicting committed entries.

Also retained as lower-severity observations:

- Commit-index persistence lags when commits advance without a new append (`{commitIndex=12, persisted=11}`).
- A restart can report `commitIndex=1` with `lastApplied=2`.
- `LogCache` does not forward `CommitTrackingLogStore`.
