What I did and found:

**Randomized partition/heal consistency stress** (`audit_fuzz_test.go`, diagnostic-only). It injects a fault *before* each round's writes (isolate one node, or a two-node block), writes to the current leader, then heals and requires convergence, asserting Raft's core invariant that every node's FSM applies the identical byte sequence. Result: 3-node × 120 rounds (225 commands) and 5-node × 80 rounds (164 commands) both converged with **no divergence, no lost or duplicated applied entries** (`audit/rand_consistency.log`).

**Cross-checked the leader loop's commit/apply and no-op dispatch, quorum/commitment accounting, RPC dispatch, and the panic-on-bad-input surface.** Nothing anomalous relative to the released implementation. The one network-reachable panic path (`DecodeConfiguration` inside `installSnapshot` for `SnapshotVersion > 0`) is longstanding upstream "panic-on-corruption" behavior between trusted peers, not a change.

**Net state of the audit:** no concrete implementation-level defect demonstrated, and none claimed. The accumulated evidence is (1) byte-identical to upstream `v1.7.3` (tag hash = the brief's `source_identity`); (2) whole-suite failures are socket-only in a second run, plus one load-induced timing flake; (3) no data races; (4) the flake does not reproduce in isolation (µs–ms convergence); (5) 200 rounds of randomized partition/heal with no FSM divergence. This is not a proof of correctness.

**Residual gaps (recorded, not hidden):** the ~15 TCP/loopback tests need the harness's private-loopback `isolated_exec` (nested `bwrap` is denied here: `Failed to create NETLINK_ROUTE socket`); and `fuzzy/` and `raft-compat/` are nested modules excluded from the Go module zip and not runnable offline (missing `raft-boltdb`; unchecked-out `raft-previous-version` submodule), so they are the only parts of the tree with no reference check.

Next useful step if given more budget: focused adversarial review of the pre-vote extension and leader-lease/leadership-transfer paths, and, if the harness exposes a sibling non-baseline variant, a diff against that to isolate the intended defect (it is not present in this baseline tree).
