**Crash/restart durability stress** (`audit_restart_test.go`, diagnostic-only). It applies commands, forces a snapshot, then restarts a follower and the leader in place (same log/stable/snapshot stores and transport, fresh FSM), requiring every node's FSM to converge to the identical applied sequence. This exercises the two paths my earlier partition-only stress did not: crash-recovery replication and snapshot restore on startup.

Result: 4/4 runs pass (~0.7–1.2 s each). Follower restart converges; leader restart triggers a new election and the old leader re-joins; the log confirms the snapshot-restore path is taken (`restored from snapshot: last-index=27`); final applied sequence length 55 (25 pre + 15 during follower downtime + 15 post). Log: `audit/restart_recovery.log`.

One-off note handled honestly: on the first pre-refactor run, the harness's own `c.Leader()` helper (waits only ≈5 s and requires exactly one leader) reported `expected one leader: []` right after the leader restart. Switching to an explicit 30 s poll made the same scenario pass in ~1 s in 4/4 runs, so I did **not** reproduce it and am treating it as a test-harness stability artifact (same class as the §3.0 flake), not a product defect — flagged as such rather than reported as a finding.

I also confirmed restart is clean by construction (`r.rpcCh = trans.Consumer()`, no forwarding goroutine survives shutdown), so no duplicate RPC consumer issue.
