## Explicit model defect claims (subject to independent verification)

### In the async actor layer, when an entry is accepted at a log index by the leader it was assigned to but is then dropped by a later leader change before decision, what resolution does the originating `append_notify` receive once the origin's local decided index reaches that index, and what does that resolution establish about the caller's entry?

Current model defect claim: defect asserted (subject to independent verification).

Current interpretation `9c8e78d93ecd43bc8cd121473003aadd-interpretation-0` · declared predicate does not hold: Current account of both mechanisms this question turned up in the actor layer's `append_notify` completion path, with the repo's own expectations as the basis for what the resolution is supposed to establish. First, the resolution arms: `drain_notifiers` answers `Ok(assigned_idx)` as soon as the local decided index reaches the index recorded at assignment, before evaluating the ballot-change arm and without comparing the caller's entry with what occupies that index, and it runs only from the actor's tick branch. A write accepted only by the leader it was assigned to, then dropped by a leader change, therefore resolves `Ok` with an index holding a different entry; the same lost-entry state resolves the documented `Superseded` instead when a tick falls between the origin's promise change and its decided-index advance, and the two arms are separated by the tick period against the re-synchronisation round trip: with a ~2 ms round trip the sweep held at 2 and 4 ms ticks and violated at 8, 16, 40 and 80 ms, and the crate's default tick period is 10 ms. Second, the assignment mapping: deferred writes are tracked as one placeholder per buffered entry, one placeholder is consumed per unit of accepted-index advance, and a placeholder is discarded while still consuming its advance when its buffered ballot no longer matches the promise; a re-synchronisation that truncates entries whose placeholders are still queued therefore shifts the index later reported, and the retained runs show the shift (index 4 while entry 33 sits at 2, or 3 while it sits at 1, the returned index holding entry 35) exactly in the configurations where the origin's two entries were still in its batch at the truncation, and no shift where the flush deadline had already removed them (two-tick deadline at 1000 or 100 ms) or where it fired before the re-synchronisation (100 and 250 ms ticks with the default hundred-tick deadline). That the returned index is supposed to identify the caller's entry is not my reading alone: the example documents the call as confirming durability of a specific entry 'decided at its assigned index' and announces the index without reading the log, and two of the runtime scenarios assert the value at the returned index - one refills an index after a lost entry and asserts the index holds the refill, the other asserts each returned index holds the entry submitted to it, with the message 'wrong-index assignment -- indices are 1-based'. Reading those two scenarios shows what they cover: the loss scenario also requires the outstanding call to fail with `Superseded` and checks the log only for the refill it then submits on the new leader, and the concurrent scenario runs against a healthy leader with no partition or leader change. The expectation is therefore encoded for the healthy path and the post-`Superseded` recovery, not for the lost-entry leader-change path, which neither scenario constructs. Both mechanisms contradict an expectation the repository itself encodes, and both end in the same caller-visible shape. A value check in `drain_notifiers` would mask both symptoms without fixing either cause: the arm order and the placeholder accounting are independent defects.
